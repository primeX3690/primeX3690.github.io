The Problem

Autonomous fleets still depend on a single point of failure

01

Centralized control

Most commercial drone / robot fleets rely on a base station or lead unit. Lose the link, lose the mission.

02

No fault tolerance

One compromised or disconnected unit can cascade into full swarm failure — unacceptable for logistics, inspection, or defense-grade operations.

03

Static formations

Existing coordination stacks struggle to re-form or reroute in real time under jamming, GNSS-denial, or unit loss.

The Solution

A swarm that coordinates without a center

Decentralized mesh coordination

Every unit can plan and act independently — no single leader, no single point of failure.

Dual intelligence modes

Switches between flocking behavior and independent decision-making depending on mission conditions.

Tamper-evident DAG ledger

Mission data replicates across the swarm via a hash-chained ledger; compromised units are auto-detected and isolated by consensus.

Real-world condition modeling

Simulates wind, battery drain, jamming, and GNSS-denial so coordination logic is stress-tested before hardware deployment.

Business Model

Phase 2 — Integration

Partner and pilot programs with existing fleet manufacturers to integrate SwarmOS as the default coordination layer, generating recurring per-fleet licensing revenue.

Phase 3 — Hardware

Move into building purpose-built drone hardware in-house, co-designing software and hardware to capture margin on both the intelligence layer and the airframe.
